# Writing rules for this branch

A short form of Chris's house style, for sessions that can't load his `house-style` and
`economist-style` skills. When a session can load those skills, they take precedence.

## Target

Plain, concrete prose that reads as if a sharp, plain-spoken engineer wrote it. The benchmark is
the Economist: short words, active voice, specific numbers, no salesmanship.

## Rules

- Australian English: organise, optimise, behaviour, vectorise.
- Every claim has a number, a name or a date behind it, or it goes.
- Active voice with a real subject: "the build leaves AVX-512 out", not "AVX-512 is excluded".
- Short words: use, help, about, enough, build.
- One idea per sentence. Vary the length: put a short sentence next to a long one.
- Numbers: one to nine in words, 10 and above in figures; "33KB", "2.8GHz", "15%".
- No serial comma unless it prevents ambiguity. Double quotes.

## AI tells to strip

- Em dashes hanging clauses off sentences. At most one per paragraph; prefer commas, full stops
  or colons.
- "Not X, but Y" and "it's not about X, it's about Y". At most one per document.
- Lists of three by reflex. Keep the strong ones; break the rest into twos or fours.
- Bold for emphasis mid-sentence. Bold is for headings and short labels only.
- Throat-clearing: "it's important to note", "at its core", "the reality is", "is real".
- Hedge clusters: "can potentially help to improve". Pick one word.
- Banned unless literal: unlock, leverage, seamless, robust, holistic, empower, streamline,
  transformative, pivotal, crucial, key (as an adjective), journey, landscape, comprehensive,
  powerful, innovative.

## The repo is public

Everything here is public, history included. See AUTOPILOT.md section 7a for what never goes in.

## Check before committing prose

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure or a press release, rewrite it.
