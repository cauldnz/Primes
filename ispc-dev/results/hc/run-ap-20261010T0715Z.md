## 2. What explains the full-occupancy lead

On the 32-vCPU node (`rust-falloff`, D32as_v7, 16 cores x 2 SMT), going from 16 to 32 threads
gave ours 7.5% (1.66M to 1.78M) and cost unpinned Rust 2.7% (1.61M to 1.56M). Pinning Rust to the
32 CPUs did not help: 1.52M pinned against 1.56M free, and 31 threads gave 1.57M. On the 96-vCPU
node the phase profile (`mt-phases-96`, an instrumented build) shows every phase about 16% slower
per pass at 24 threads and 41% to 47% slower at 48, against one thread. From 48 to 96 threads the dense
phase gains 14% per core while the sparse phase loses 8%, at 94.5k and 195.9k cycles per pass per
thread. With the other 47 cores spinning (`loaded-1t-96`), one pinned thread loses 25%, ours and
Rust alike. With the other 47 cores running a Rust sieve (`loaded-1t-sieve-96`), it loses 29%
(ours) and 28% (Rust), close to the 32% per-thread drop the 48-thread profile implies. A busy SMT
sibling costs one thread 32% (spin) and 45% (sieve) for ours, against 30% and 50% for Rust. The
sieve-load rows in `loaded-1t-96` are left out: the load never ran (EVENTS.jsonl, 08:58), and those
rows sit within 3% of idle.
